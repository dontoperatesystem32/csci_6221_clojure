# Clojure Team – Group Governing Rules

**Team Members:**
1. Rufat Guliyev
2. William Switzer

Our team will work on the Clojure programming language throughout the semester. To keep our collaboration organized and avoid misunderstandings, we agreed on the following rules.

### Collaboration Method

We will use a combination of **asynchronous and synchronous collaboration**.

Most development work, research, documentation, and smaller tasks will be completed asynchronously. We will communicate through direct-message texting and use Git/GitHub to share and review project code.

For tasks that require discussion, such as making important project decisions, dividing responsibilities, solving difficult problems, or preparing presentations, we will work synchronously during our weekly meeting.

### Weekly Meeting Time

We agreed to meet and work together every **Sunday from 6:00 PM to 8:00 PM**.

If one team member cannot attend the scheduled meeting, they should inform the other member as early as possible and agree on another suitable time.

### Participation and Responsibilities

Both team members are expected to contribute approximately equally to the project. At the beginning of each major task, we will divide the work and clearly establish who is responsible for each part.

Both members should:
- Attend the agreed weekly meetings.
- Complete assigned tasks by the agreed deadlines.
- Regularly communicate their progress.
- Inform the other team member if they encounter a problem or expect a delay.
- Review important project decisions together.
- Understand the overall project, including parts primarily implemented by the other team member.

### Communication

We will communicate primarily through direct-message texting for quick discussions, progress updates, and coordination. Important decisions regarding project architecture, implementation, presentation, or assignment requirements will be discussed and agreed upon by both members.

We aim to respond to project-related messages within **24 hours**, unless there is an exceptional situation.

### Code and Project Management

We will use **Git and GitHub** to manage the source code of the project. Each member will work on their assigned tasks and regularly commit their changes.

Before making major changes that affect the other team member's work, we will discuss them together. Both members are responsible for keeping the repository organized and ensuring that the final project works correctly.

### Conflict Resolution

If we disagree about a technical or organizational decision, we will discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each option and try to reach an agreement based on what is best for the project.

If a serious problem cannot be resolved within the team, we will contact the professor or grader for guidance.

Both team members agree to follow these rules throughout the semester and to communicate openly if any of the rules need to be adjusted.
